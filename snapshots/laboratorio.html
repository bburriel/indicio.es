Could it be a cause?

Two figures moving together does not prove that one causes the other. Here is what you can check with registered unemployment and income per person, from the least to the most demanding test.

Explained by something elseHolds up poorlyWithstands almost everythingPasses every test
Withstands almost everythingIt survives almost every explanation we can measure. It is worth investigating with a before-and-after comparison.
Compare municipalitiesExceeds 4 of 5

Exceeds 4 of 5 tests:

  • ✅Among similar municipalities. Comparing municipalities of similar size and age, the relationship even gets stronger (from −0.49 to −0.56).
  • ❌Within each province. It holds in 22 of 49 provinces. Comparing only within the same province, the relationship weakens sharply (−0.22).
  • ✅In the changes too. Comparing changes instead of levels (change in registered unemployment since 2015 versus rise in income since 2015), the relationship holds (r = −0.22).
  • ✅Twin municipalities. In 7 out of every 10 twin pairs (972 pairs), the one with more registered unemployment has less income per person.
  • ✅It rises steadily. From one group to the next, income per person moves in the expected direction in 4 of 4 steps.
Prime suspectThe province. Comparing only municipalities in the same province, the relationship goes from −0.49 to −0.22. A good part of it reflects the difference between one part of Spain and another.

What would have to be true for it to be a cause
  1. That nothing we have not measured moves at the same time registered unemployment and income per person. Here only size, age, income, education and province can be netted out; everything else (history, climate, roads, political decisions) stays in.
  2. That it does not run the other way round. ↺ Perhaps it is not registered unemployment that moves income per person, but income per person that attracts registered unemployment. Data from a single moment cannot tell you: you need to see what changed beforehand.
  3. That what holds for municipalities holds for people, if the question is about people. Municipalities with more of one thing having more of another does not mean every resident fits that pattern.
  4. That the controls do not spoil the comparison. Controlling for something that lies on the causal path, or that is a result, can erase or create a relationship. How to choose them →

Before drawing conclusions

  • Two things going together does not mean one causes the other. Municipalities with more education have higher incomes, but other factors could explain it: big cities, type of job, age.
  • These are data on municipalities, not on people. A party winning more votes where there are more older people does not mean older people vote for it. This is known as the ecological fallacy.
  • Very small towns swing a lot. With 200 residents, five families can shift any percentage. That is why municipalities with fewer than 1,000 inhabitants are excluded by default.
  • The years do not always match. The census is from 2021, income from 2024, the register from 2025 and the vote from 2023. The sources of each figure appear below the chart.
  • r only measures straight-line relationships. If the chart shows a curve, r can understate the link. A logarithmic scale helps when a few values are very high.

Where the data come from

INE: Household Income Distribution Atlas 2015–2024 (the years 2015–2023 through the open project ineAtlas.data). INE: municipal register 1996–2025. INE: Population and Housing Census 2021 and annual population censuses 2021–2025. INE: Migration and Change of Residence Statistics 2021–2024. INE: Rental Housing Price Index (rents declared to the tax authority), by municipality and, by age of the contract, by province. INE: measurement of tourist dwellings (August 2020 to May 2026). INE: Wage Structure Survey. INE: IPC. SEPE: registered unemployment by municipality, 2015–September 2026. Ministry of the Interior: general elections of 2019 and 2023, municipal elections of 2019 and 2023 and European elections of 2024. idealista: average price of rental listings. Eurostat: monthly harmonised index of consumer prices by food group (prc_hicp_midx), 2021–2025, Spain, Portugal, France, Italy and Germany. Distances to the nearest city and to the coast calculated by Indicio as the crow flies on IGN mapping. INE: Consumer Price Index by region (general and groups, since 2002) and by subclass. INE: Household Budget Survey (average spending per household and per person on food, 2016–2025). Eurostat (national accounts, European labour force survey, public accounts and HICP, since 1995) and the World Bank (development indicators, before 1995). INE: Housing Price Index, by region. Ministry of Housing: appraised value of non-subsidised housing, non-subsidised housing starts and completions, subsidised housing. INE: Living Conditions Survey, households by tenure and income. Eurostat: house prices, housing cost overburden, young people leaving home, housing investment and households. INE: Annual Wage Structure Survey (percentiles of annual earnings and minimum wage bands). INE: labour force survey, main-job wages by decile (with tax data). INE: Living Conditions Survey, income deciles per consumption unit. INE: Quarterly Labour Cost Survey, wage cost per worker and month, by region. Ministry of Finance: outstanding debt of each town council at 31 December, 2015–2025 (excluding 2017). SEPE: initial permanent and temporary contracts by municipality and month (2005–2026) and registered unemployment by municipality. Social Security: contributors by municipality, scheme and activity, December 2011 to 2025. Ministry of Housing: home sales by municipality and quarter (notaries), 2004–2026. Ministry of Housing: subsidised housing with final certification (state and regional plans, 1991–2025), non-subsidised housing starts (1991–2025) and notarial home transactions (2004–2025).

How to read them. Income comes from tax returns and does not include the informal economy. Unless it says «adjusted for inflation», euros are the current euros of each year. Province and Spain figures are population-weighted averages of their municipalities. For very small municipalities, INE does not publish all the data. The exact calculation behind each label («at equal age», «among equals», «explains») is in the method guide, and the code on GitHub.
